**[Website: www.yanquan.org](https://www.yanquan.org)** | English | [简体中文](README.CN.md) | [Windows version](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-ime) | [macOS version](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-ime-mac)

Cassotis IME for Linux (言泉输入法 Linux 版) is an experimental Chinese Pinyin input method for Linux desktops. Native IBus and Fcitx 5 adapters share one engine service written in Free Pascal, ported from [Cassotis IME for Windows](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-ime), with dictionaries generated by [Cassotis Lexicon](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-lexicon). Candidate ranking runs entirely on the local CPU, around a single character-level language model trained by the project. It shows no ads, bundles no software and uploads nothing you type: dictionaries, settings and what it learns stay on the computer.

Version 2.7.0 aligns with Cassotis IME and Cassotis Lexicon v2.7.0. What each release brought is in the [version history](#version-history) below and in the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md); packages are available from [Releases](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-ime-linux/releases).

The project focus is:
- keep the Linux engine aligned with each Windows release and verify the alignment on the same benchmarks,
- integrate natively with IBus and Fcitx 5 on GNOME, KDE and other desktops,
- ship amd64 and arm64 packages that run without network access or a GPU.

## Name Origin
The English name **Cassotis** comes from the sacred spring inside the Temple of Delphi. Before delivering oracles, the priestess Pythia was said to drink from this spring to enter a prophetic state. The spring was regarded as the true source of prophecy and inspiration, where oracles were born, which resonates with the path from Delphi to human language.

The Chinese name **言泉** (Yanquan, "Spring of Words") matches Cassotis as a prophetic spring, while also carrying the meaning of **言如泉涌** ("words flowing like a spring"), reflecting our expectation of a fluent and intelligent input experience.

## Features
- **IBus and Fcitx 5.** Both adapters talk to one shared engine service, so they use the same settings, dictionaries and learned words. Only one framework needs to be active in a desktop session.
- **Candidate panel.** Candidates are drawn by IBus or Fcitx 5 and follow the desktop theme; the page holds 3 to 9 candidates (9 by default) and can be paged with `-/+`, `[/]`, `,/.` or `Shift+Tab/Tab`. Appearance settings offer automatic, fixed vertical or fixed horizontal layout; see [Candidate Layout](CONFIGURATION.md#candidates).
- **English recognition.** In Chinese full-Pinyin, half-width mode, letters that are an English word or name (`pro`, `markdown`, `Effie`), that are typed with a capital, or that were committed as letters before appear as an unnumbered candidate before the numbered ones. When the input has no Chinese reading as a whole they are highlighted and Space commits them as typed; when it has one (`bug`, `mac`), the language model weighs the letters against it with the preceding text. Chinese selection numbers stay unchanged, and raw text is never learned as a Chinese word. Enabled by default and optional in Settings; Double Pinyin keeps its normal behavior.
- **One-key completion.** Exactly one trusted continuation is shown, separately from the candidates, and accepted with `Tab` or the backtick key, whichever is configured. Dictionary and strong-transition completions appear at once; the language model reranks or extends them in the background and shows nothing when its confidence is insufficient.
- **Poetry completion.** Type the beginning of a stored line to complete it; a full line can offer the next line of the poem, including its punctuation, instead of a generic generated suffix.
- **Long sentences.** Lexicon-constrained path search builds complete candidates (for example `womenjintian` → `我们今天`) while keeping prefix words and single characters selectable for partial commitment. The language model chooses among the complete candidates and then corrects small homophone errors against the typed Pinyin and the text before the input.
- **Short words.** The language model and general-text word frequencies resolve competing exact candidates, using the text already committed before the cursor when there is any. Without context, a much rarer word cannot displace a strongly supported first choice solely on model preference, and a first choice that everyday conversation uses far more often keeps its place. After preceding text, the characters of a single syllable are also ordered by the language model.
- **Traditional input.** Candidates and committed text use Taiwan traditional forms. The language model reads simplified equivalents of candidates and preceding text, including in background completion, so traditional-only characters are not penalized merely for their written form.
- **Short phrases.** An input of three to five syllables that the dictionary does not contain (for example `jiuguize` → `旧规则`) gets whole phrases put together from dictionary words. The language model picks one, with the preceding text when there is any, and corrects its homophones against the typed Pinyin; a phrase picked from behind the first one is learned.
- **Pinyin schemes.** Full Pinyin, initial-letter abbreviations (`jt` → `今天`) and seven Double Pinyin schemes (Microsoft, Xiaohe, Ziranma, Sogou, Ziguang, Pinyin Jiajia and Zhineng ABC) share ranking and learning data. Eleven fuzzy-Pinyin pairs can be enabled individually. `lue`/`nue` are accepted for `lve`/`nve` and `jv`/`qv`/`xv` for `ju`/`qu`/`xu`; an apostrophe separates syllables unless the optional second/third selection keys are enabled.
- **Second/third selection keys.** Optionally use `;` and `'` to select the second and third visible candidates. Disabled by default; Double Pinyin keeps semicolon as a final-code key inside a pair. See [Selection Keys](CONFIGURATION.md#secondthird-selection-keys).
- **Dictionaries and learning.** Separate simplified and traditional base dictionaries and one user dictionary. Learned words follow the selected simplified or traditional display through OpenCC; select a learned candidate and press `Ctrl+Delete` to remove it.
- **Document-local adaptation.** Text that the framework exposes around the cursor makes recurring terms and continuations easier to reach. It stays in memory, is isolated per input context and is never written to disk.
- **Personal word frequencies.** Import your own word-frequency list from Advanced settings as CSV, TSV or a text export. Entries use the same learning checks as normal selections; imported preferences are shared by both frameworks. See [Importing Word Frequencies](CONFIGURATION.md#importing-word-frequencies) for formats and precautions.
- **Quick input.** `ufh` symbols, `uxh` numbered markers, `usx` mathematical symbols, `ujt` arrows, `udw` units, `ubd` Chinese punctuation, `urq`/`usj`/`uxq` the current date, time and weekday; `i123.45` or `v123.45` converts Chinese numbers and uppercase currency amounts. Double Pinyin enters these modes with `Shift+U/I/V`. See [Quick Input](CONFIGURATION.md#quick-input).
- **Chinese punctuation.** With Chinese punctuation and half-width input, a period right after typed digits becomes a decimal point, and the symbols that stay as typed can be edited in Settings; the default Markdown preset keeps ``-#*+_=`~[]<>|@`` while commas, full stops, parentheses and quotation marks stay Chinese.
- **Settings.** A GTK 3 application for the options supported on Linux; every function shortcut can be switched off without losing its key binding.
- **Custom phrases.** Define a letter shortcut for a literal phrase, address or multiline text. An exact shortcut appears in the completion row and is committed with the configured `Tab` or backtick key, without changing numbered candidates or learning the phrase. Settings supports ordered editing and JSON import/export; diagnostics are grouped under Advanced. See [Custom Phrases](CONFIGURATION.md#custom-phrases).
- **Local by design.** No network and no GPU. Dictionary candidates are available from the first key; while the language model is loading or unavailable, candidates are ranked without it.

## Download and Install

Download packages and their checksums from [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-ime-linux/releases). Every package contains both the IBus and the Fcitx 5 adapter, the dictionaries and the language model. Choose the installation method for your system first:

| System | Download | Installation method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Debian, Ubuntu and derivatives with compatible dependencies | `.deb` | [APT installation](#debian-installation) |
| SteamOS Desktop Mode and other systems with read-only system directories | `.tar.gz` | [User installation, without sudo](#user-installation) |
| Other distributions with compatible libraries and writable system directories | `.tar.gz` | [System-wide portable installation](#system-installation) |

The portable archives contain the same dynamically linked binaries as the matching `.deb` and need compatible runtime libraries; they are not distribution-independent packages. Run the portable installer's dependency preflight first, and build from source if the supplied binaries are incompatible. Learned words and settings are kept by every upgrade and every uninstall method.

<a id="debian-installation"></a>

### Debian / Ubuntu: APT Installation

Download the `.deb` matching the system architecture, compare its SHA-256 value with the digest shown in the release asset metadata, and install it with APT:

```bash
arch="$(dpkg --print-architecture)"  # amd64 or arm64
package="cassotis-ime_2.7.0_${arch}.deb"
sha256sum "${package}"
sudo apt install "./${package}"
```

The installer refreshes active IBus and Fcitx 5 desktop sessions. On GNOME with IBus, Cassotis is added to the input-source list without a reboot or a new login; the current input source is not changed. If no graphical session is active during installation, Cassotis is discovered at the next login. Press `Super+Space` and select **Cassotis 言泉拼音输入法** to start typing.

For an Fcitx 5 session, select Fcitx 5 as the desktop input-method framework and add Cassotis with the Fcitx 5 configuration tool. IBus and Fcitx 5 may coexist, but only one framework should control the current desktop session.

Open settings from the Cassotis preferences action in GNOME's input-source settings or from the Cassotis configuration action in `fcitx5-configtool`; the fallback command is `/usr/libexec/cassotis-ime/cassotis-settings`. Upgrade by installing a newer package the same way; remove it with `sudo apt remove cassotis-ime`.

Upgrades refresh the input framework. If an application that was already open stops accepting Chinese input, save any unsent text, then close it normally and reopen it to reconnect. There is no need to delete your user dictionary or reinstall the system.

<a id="user-installation"></a>

### SteamOS And Other Read-Only Systems: User Installation

Download the portable archive matching `uname -m` (`x86_64` or `aarch64`) and verify its SHA-256 value. In SteamOS **Desktop Mode**, or another desktop with read-only system directories, install into your personal directories. Do not reuse an old install script or run `sudo ./install.sh`. Finish any pending composition, then open a terminal as the current desktop user and run these commands **without `sudo` or `su`**:

```bash
arch="$(uname -m)"  # x86_64 or aarch64
archive="cassotis-ime-linux-2.7.0-${arch}.tar.gz"
sha256sum "${archive}"
tar -xzf "${archive}"
cd "cassotis-ime-linux-2.7.0-${arch}"
./install.sh --user --check &&
./install.sh --user
```

The `--check` command only performs preflight checks; the installation runs only after they pass. On KDE, automatic selection prefers an installed Fcitx 5; on GNOME it prefers IBus. Use `--framework fcitx5` or `--framework ibus` to choose, and pass the same option to both commands. The installer checks the architecture, shared-library and ABI dependencies and the framework version before copying files. It installs the runtime under `~/.local/libexec/cassotis-ime` and the dictionaries under `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/cassotis-ime`, without writing to `/usr` or disabling filesystem protection. The framework must already be installed and enabled in the desktop session; the installer does not install dependencies or switch frameworks.

Select Cassotis in the active framework after installation. If its list has not refreshed, restart that framework or sign out and back in. The fallback settings command is `~/.local/libexec/cassotis-ime/cassotis-settings`.

The portable bundle includes an isolated OpenCC fallback for systems without the conversion library or data; it does not replace system libraries. User installation, upgrade, removal and desktop input were tested on SteamOS 3.8.14, KDE X11 with IBus 1.5.32; SteamOS Gaming Mode and Fcitx 5 on SteamOS were not. If preflight reports incompatible libraries or an older Fcitx version, use a build for that distribution rather than disabling its filesystem protection. See [user installation and removal](BUILD.md#portable-user-installation).

To upgrade, extract the new archive and repeat the preflight and installation commands as the same desktop user. To remove, run `./uninstall.sh --user` **without sudo**; it removes only tracked, unmodified package files. Do not layer system, source-user and portable-user installations.

<a id="system-installation"></a>

### Other Systems: System-Wide Portable Installation

Use this method only with writable system directories, administrator privileges and compatible runtime libraries. For SteamOS and other read-only systems, use the [user installation](#user-installation) instead.

**Do not use system installation from v2.1.0 or older portable archives.** Their installer can overwrite system-directory ownership, permissions and SELinux labels, preventing desktop login. Use a v2.5.0 or later portable archive, or user installation instead. The corrected installer includes `install-support/system_install.py`; it preserves existing directory attributes and requires `restorecon` (usually provided by `policycoreutils`) when SELinux is enabled.

Inside an extracted, fixed portable archive:

```bash
if test -r install-support/system_install.py; then
    sudo ./install.sh --system
else
    printf 'Use a fixed portable installer or --user installation.\n' >&2
fi
```

The portable installer does not resolve runtime dependencies; [BUILD.md](BUILD.md) lists the required libraries. Remove this installation with `sudo ./uninstall.sh`.

The fix prevents new damage; it does not guess or reset system-directory attributes damaged by an older installer. If login is already affected, recover those attributes using the distribution's recovery tools before reinstalling. Disabling SELinux or recursively changing permissions is not a substitute.

## Architecture
- `src/engine`: Pinyin parsing, candidate generation, ranking and learning, ported from the Windows engine.
- `src/host`: language-model inference, correction and background completion; `src/host/native` holds the C++20 bridge to ONNX Runtime and the model's attention operator.
- `src/service`: the shared engine service (`cassotis-engine`) behind both frameworks, with its Unix socket server and idle-memory release.
- `src/ipc`: the protocol between the adapters and the engine service.
- `src/dictionary`, `src/common`: SQLite access, the user dictionary, configuration and platform utilities.
- `adapters/ibus`, `adapters/fcitx5`, `adapters/common`: the two framework adapters and the GTK 3 settings application.
- `data`: the dictionary schema and the language model (`data/models/char_lm`, stored as numbered parts).
- `third_party/onnxruntime`: the CPU ONNX Runtime 1.20.1 libraries for x86_64 and aarch64.
- `scripts`, `packaging`: build, installation and packaging scripts.
- `tools/control`: the service-control client.

## Runtime Files
- `cassotis-engine`: the shared engine service; it loads the dictionaries and the language model.
- `ibus-engine-cassotis`: the IBus adapter.
- `libcassotis.so`: the Fcitx 5 addon.
- `cassotis-settings`, `cassotis-control`: the settings application and the service-control client.
- `libcassotis_pinyin_transformer_ort.so`, `libonnxruntime.so.1.20.1` and `char_lm/`: the inference bridge, the runtime and the model files. Only the engine service loads them; the adapters never do.
- `dict_sc.db`, `dict_tc.db`: the base dictionaries, under `/usr/share/cassotis-ime/` for system packages or `~/.local/share/cassotis-ime/` for user installations.

Without the engine service no framework can convert input. Without the model files input still works, but candidates are ranked without the language model.

## Build (Quick Start)
Requirements: Linux x86_64 or aarch64, Free Pascal 3.2.2, C/C++20 compilers, SQLite 3, OpenCC, Python 3 with PyGObject and GTK 3, and the IBus and Fcitx 5 development headers. CMake, CUDA, a GPU and a system ONNX Runtime are not needed.

```bash
./scripts/check_environment.sh
./rebuild_all.sh
```

`rebuild_all.sh` rebuilds the engine service, both adapters, the control client and the native inference bridge into `build/bin/`, and joins the numbered parts in `data/models/char_lm` into the `char_lm.onnx` the runtime loads, after checking their SHA-256 values. To install a source build for the current desktop user, obtain matching `dict_sc.db` and `dict_tc.db` from [Cassotis Lexicon](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-lexicon) and run, without sudo:

```bash
./scripts/install_ibus.sh --dictionary /path/to/dict_sc.db \
  --dictionary-traditional /path/to/dict_tc.db
# or
./scripts/install_fcitx5.sh --dictionary /path/to/dict_sc.db \
  --dictionary-traditional /path/to/dict_tc.db
```

Do not layer such an installation over a Debian package. See [BUILD.md](BUILD.md) for the options of these scripts and release packaging.

## Dictionary Workflow
The base dictionaries are the schema-24 SQLite databases of [Cassotis Lexicon](https://github.com/shenmin/cassotis-lexicon), built from its generated artifacts:
- the word lists `dict_unihan_sc.txt`, `dict_unihan_tc.txt`, `dict_clean_sc.txt` and `dict_clean_tc.txt`,
- the tables derived from corpora in the same directory: word-transition priors, completion priors, general-text and conversational word frequencies, and the short-word first-choice table.

The v2.7.0 dictionaries hold 264,167 simplified and 267,265 traditional reading records, including 23,919 and 24,182 single-character records respectively; a word with several readings has several records. Their word-frequency tables cover 201,868 simplified and 204,020 traditional texts; separate conversational-frequency tables cover 106,115 and 107,002 texts. They include reviewed classical poetry lines, corrected polyphonic readings, and consistent Taiwan traditional forms across the word and ranking tables. The user dictionary is `~/.local/share/cassotis-ime/user_dict.db`; base dictionaries belonging to the running installation take precedence over old copies from another installation. [docs/DICTIONARY.md](docs/DICTIONARY.md) describes the format and explicit path overrides.

## Models and Ranking

### The language model
- **One model for every input path.** A character-level autoregressive language model of about 150 million parameters (11 layers, 1,024 dimensions, a 12,017-character vocabulary, 8-bit quantized), trained by the project. It scores complete long-sentence candidates and corrects the chosen sentence against the typed syllables, chooses among short words and orders single characters after a context, composes phrases outside the dictionary, weighs typed letters against a Chinese reading, reranks one-key completions, and proposes and selects long-sentence Tab continuations. It is the model of Cassotis IME for Windows; model files of earlier releases are removed during upgrades.
- **Training data.** Independent Chinese corpora (general text and fiction), both as a plain language model and conditioned on Pinyin. Since 2.7.0 it has also learned everyday conversation: its training continued on the tenth of the public LCCC dialogue corpus closest to everyday chat, and the result was averaged with the previous model, so that fiction and general text keep their scores. No benchmark case is in any training data.
- **Scores that do not depend on the call.** The model is quantized with fixed activation ranges and computes attention with an operator of its own, so a text gets the same scores however it is batched and whatever was computed before. A long sentence typed key by key gets the same candidates as the same Pinyin entered at once.
- **Memory.** In the v2.0.0 tests, the engine service settled at about 311 MiB on x86_64 and 320 MiB on aarch64 after loading and five idle seconds, against about 437 MiB in 1.0.0, when it carried the separate models of Windows v1.31.0. These are historical idle-memory measurements, separate from the current benchmark peaks.

### Decisions around the model
- Six small gradient-boosted policies turn language-model scores into decisions: long-sentence choice, context-free short-word choice, contextual short-word choice, context-detail scoring, one-key completion and Tab continuation. They are compiled in with the same parameters as on Windows and add no neural model files or inference sessions.
- Three compact structural rankers support long-sentence search and the pool of complete candidates.
- Statistical tables from the lexicon add what the model does not see by itself: word transitions, completion priors, a short-word promotion table precomputed for the dictionary, and how often general text and everyday conversation use each word.

### How candidates are ranked
- **Long sentences.** Lexicon-constrained search builds complete candidates; two rankers decide which paths survive pruning and a third orders the pool of complete candidates. The language model scores the leading candidates and the long-sentence policy picks the first and the second. The model then checks the chosen sentence against the typed syllables and up to 48 preceding characters to repair small homophone errors, with a bounded second pass when needed. This works for simplified/traditional input and Full/Double Pinyin; user words and exact full-query dictionary matches are left alone.
- **Short words.** The precomputed promotion table establishes the dictionary order. The short-word policy then considers language-model scores and general-text word frequencies, consulting the preceding text when the context-free decision is not strong enough. Without context, a word at least ten times rarer than the first choice cannot replace it solely on model preference. Strong conversational-frequency evidence can also retain the first choice when general-text frequency and dictionary weight support it. Single-character choices can use preceding text. Personal choices remain separate from these base-dictionary signals.
- **Phrases outside the dictionary.** For an input of three to five syllables that is neither a dictionary entry nor a user word, the dictionary supplies whole-phrase drafts for every way of cutting the input. The language model scores each by how well it stands as a clause of its own and picks one; it then corrects the chosen draft against the typed syllables. This also runs for Double Pinyin and traditional input; fuzzy Pinyin and an explicitly typed syllable separator `'` keep their separate candidate paths.
- **English letters.** Where typed letters are also a Chinese reading, the language model compares the letters with the page's first readings in one call, with the preceding text; the letters go first only with a clear lead.
- **One-key completion.** Dictionary and strong-transition completions are shown at once. The language model reranks a wider set of dictionary words in the background and replaces the shown completion only when the gain is clear.
- **Tab continuation in long sentences.** The language model proposes the next characters and scores the ways to finish the current word or continue with the next phrase; the Tab policy picks one or shows nothing.

### Runtime behavior
- Everything is computed locally on the CPU with the bundled ONNX Runtime; no network and no GPU are used.
- Only the engine service loads the model, in the background. Ordinary candidates, selection, commitment and static completion do not wait for it.
- A foreground model call has a 100 ms deadline and a background completion 120 ms; a late result is dropped and the existing result stays.
- The model uses one thread per available CPU, at most six on x86_64 and eight on aarch64.
- After five seconds without input the service returns unused heap pages without unloading the model or losing context.

## Benchmarks

The `v2.7.0` rows are native measurements of the current engine and dictionaries; earlier rows retain their original measurements. See [Compatibility](COMPATIBILITY.md) for the scope of framework and package checks. Since v2.0.0, these corpus benchmarks have no wall-clock search, model or completion deadline: accuracy and latency come from the same completed query, with eight model threads. Deterministic search bounds are unchanged.
Test methods, the corpus and the scoring rules are in [BENCHMARK.md](BENCHMARK.md) ([Chinese](BENCHMARK.CN.md)). The cases are those of Cassotis IME for Windows, taken from the developer's own novel [**Elegance in Timelessness** (永恒的舞动)](https://www.qidian.com/book/1037259117/) and kept separate from all training data.

The 2.7.0 results were measured on 2026-11-11 (UTC+8), on Ubuntu 26.04.1 VMs with eight vCPUs: x86_64 has 16 GiB on an Intel Core i5-12600K host, and aarch64 has 8 GiB on Apple silicon. Versions 2.6.0, 2.5.0, 2.1.0 and 2.0.0 were measured on 2026-11-03, 2026-10-29, 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-17 on the same machines. These are not controlled hardware comparisons. Versions 2.7.0 through 2.1.0 use the same corrected cases, including 11,685 contested short-word cases. Earlier sets had 11,728 contested cases and are not paired case-for-case comparisons. Latencies are in ms.

The installed input method still uses foreground 100 ms and background 120 ms safeguards. These deadline-free scores do not claim identical results under production deadlines: the earlier timed x86_64 long run scored 37 fewer Top1 and 36 fewer Top2. That result has not been erased or fixed by changing the benchmark protocol. Full results, timing scopes and historical measurements are retained in [BENCHMARK.md](BENCHMARK.md).

## Configuration
Settings are kept by the shared engine service, so IBus and Fcitx 5 see the same state. Open **Cassotis IME Settings** from the application list or from the framework's Cassotis menu.

Important options include:
- input mode (simplified Chinese, traditional Chinese, English) and Pinyin scheme
- individual fuzzy-Pinyin pairs
- punctuation style and character width
- whether a full stop typed after digits becomes a decimal point
- English recognition in Chinese mode, on by default
- symbols kept half-width in Chinese punctuation, the Markdown preset by default
- candidate layout: automatic, fixed vertical or fixed horizontal
- `;` and `'` for the second and third candidate, off by default
- candidate page size, paging and completion keys, and individually switchable function shortcuts

User data follows the XDG base-directory specification: `~/.local/share/cassotis-ime/user_dict.db` and the service socket `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/cassotis-ime/engine.sock`. See [CONFIGURATION.md](CONFIGURATION.md) for every option.

## Roadmap
- follow each Windows release of the engine, the language model and the dictionaries, and keep the parity checks and the shared benchmarks green
- shorten the time from a keystroke to its candidates, above all for long sentences on x86_64
- test more distributions, desktops and applications, including Chromium and Electron programs, mixed-DPI setups, SteamOS Gaming Mode and Fcitx 5 on SteamOS
- widen the binary compatibility of the portable archives and their dependency preflight
- keep idle memory and cold-start responsiveness within their release gates

## Version History
The milestones of the Linux port, above all of its models. Each Linux release follows a release of Cassotis IME for Windows, named in brackets; every release's full changes are in the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md).

**v0.1.0 (Windows v1.17.0): the first release.** One shared Free Pascal engine with short-word, long-sentence, completion, context-ranking and user-learning paths; native IBus and Fcitx 5 adapters over the same engine, settings and dictionaries; a GTK 3 settings application; amd64 and arm64 Debian packages and portable archives.

**v0.2.0 – v0.9.1 (Windows v1.18.0 – v1.29.0): the neural models of Windows.** v0.2.0 brings all 42 generated ranking models and a native C++20/ONNX Runtime bridge for the first Transformer reranker. Later releases port the Pinyin-conditioned candidate scorer (v0.3.0), the constrained generators and document-local adaptation (v0.4.0), Pinyin-constrained local sentence repair with an INT8 model (v0.6.0), joint repair (v0.8.0) and context-aware short-word disambiguation with INT8 early-exit models (v0.9.0). v0.8.0 also adds user installation without sudo for SteamOS and other read-only systems, and v0.9.1 lowers resident memory with compact caches, file-backed model weights and the release of unused memory after five idle seconds.

**v1.0.0 (Windows v1.31.0): a shared character language model.** It replaces the RBT3 short-context models for long-sentence ranking, contextual short words and completion; short completion is reranked in the background, and long completion is chosen by a learned policy over dictionary tails, next-word recall and predicted characters.

**v2.0.0 (Windows v2.2.0): one language model.** One character language model and seven compact units replace the separate models, and retired model files are removed during upgrades. Phrases of three to five syllables outside the dictionary are composed by the model and corrected against the typed Pinyin. English recognition in Chinese mode, U/I/V quick input and ordered custom phrases arrive in the same release.

**v2.1.0 (Windows v2.4.0): traditional Chinese through the model.** Traditional candidates and context are scored through a simplified view of the model, while candidates, corrections and background completion keep Taiwan's character forms. Personal word frequencies exported by other input methods can be imported.

**v2.5.0 (Windows v2.5.0): the word frequencies of general text.** The short-word policy is retrained with general-text word frequencies, so that common words come first without context, and word transitions are loaded per query. The candidate layout becomes selectable: automatic, fixed vertical or fixed horizontal. From this release the Linux version follows the Windows version number.

**v2.6.0 (Windows v2.6.0): two models decide a correction.** Homophone corrections of long sentences are judged by the Pinyin-conditioned and the whole-sentence scores together, with bounded dictionary-word alternatives, and contextual and context-free short words get policies of their own.

**v2.7.0 (Windows v2.7.0): everyday conversation.** The language model continued its training on everyday dialogues and its six policies were refit; how often conversation uses a word keeps common first choices without context. Phrase generation and correction run for traditional and Double Pinyin input, single characters after a context are ordered by the model, and Zhineng ABC joins the Double Pinyin schemes.
